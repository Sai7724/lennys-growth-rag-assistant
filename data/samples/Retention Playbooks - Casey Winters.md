Title: Retention Playbooks
Guest: Casey Winters
Timestamp: 00:12:30

Casey Winters on retention: The first mistake teams make is optimizing activation
before retention. If new users come back, they churn for a product reason, not a
growth reason. You fix retention by improving the core value loop, not by adding
notifications. He explains how Airbnb doubled its repeat booking rate by
shortening the time between a booking and the next meaningful interaction on the
platform. The playbook: identify the highest-frequency moment of value, remove
friction from that path, and instrument a "return to product" metric so every
sprint has a retention north star. He also warns that cohort curves that look
flat are often masking a bad new-user experience, and that you should segment
by user age (days since signup), not by calendar month, to see the real signal.
For PLG products specifically, he recommends tracking the "aha session" — the
first session where a user performs the core action — and measuring the rate at
which users reach it within their first seven days. Teams that move that rate
from 20 percent to 40 percent typically see proportional improvements in D30
retention. He closes with a warning: retention work is invisible work. Churn is
the slow leak that no demo day celebrates, but fixing it compounds more value
than any acquisition campaign ever will.
