Title: PLG Loops That Compound
Guest: Elena Verna
Timestamp: 00:41:05

Elena Verna on product-led growth: A growth loop is only worth building if it
compounds — the output of one cycle feeds the next. She explains the classic
invite loop: a user invites a teammate, the teammate converts, and the expanded
team drives more usage, which drives more invites. The loop is capped when the
inviter has no more teammates to invite, so you need a second loop running in
parallel, like a content loop or an integration loop. She talks about pricing as
a growth lever: tiering should map to a team's adoption stage, not to seat
counts. Enterprise teams often need a monthly active user based tier before they
will ever consider an annual contract. She also covers the cold start problem:
before any loop exists, founders should manually do the thing they eventually
automate, and measure the conversion rate of the manual motion first. If the
manual motion converts at under one percent, do not automate the loop. Her
warning for founders: most teams measure the wrong loop stage. They track signups
when they should track the first value moment, because a signup that never
reaches the value moment is not a user, it is a cost.
